# ChatGPT Image Prompt — Avighnn Global Product Segment Photos

**How to use:** open ChatGPT (with image generation), paste everything below the
horizontal line into a single message, and send it. It will produce four separate
images, one per product segment. If it stops after the first, reply "next image"
until all four are done. Save them as `architectural.jpg`, `industrial.jpg`,
`custom-dies.jpg`, `standard-shapes.jpg`.

---

You are a commercial product photographer and art director shooting a catalogue for
an aluminium extrusion exporter. I need images for my company website.

## ABOUT THE COMPANY

Avighnn Global is an aluminium extrusion exporter based in Meerut, India, supplying
precision aluminium profiles and extrusions to buyers worldwide. The website sells
four distinct supply lines. Buyers land on the site and need to recognise, within a
second or two, whether we make the kind of product they are sourcing. The images are
the recognition cue — each one must instantly say "this is the category you want."

## WHAT I NEED — READ THIS CAREFULLY

Generate **FOUR (4) SEPARATE, INDIVIDUAL IMAGES** — one image per product segment,
each generated as its own standalone image.

**Do NOT create one image containing four panels, quadrants, sections, collages,
grids, or split-screens.** Do not put multiple segments in one frame. Do not create
a composite or a contact sheet of four. I need four distinct image outputs that I
can download separately and place on four different cards on my website.

Generate image 1, then image 2, then image 3, then image 4, as separate generations.

## SHARED STYLE — APPLY IDENTICALLY TO ALL FOUR IMAGES

The four images must look like one photo shoot, same day, same studio, same lens,
so they sit side by side on a web page without clashing.

- **Medium:** photorealistic commercial product photography. Sharp, clean, high
  detail. Not illustration, not a 3D render look, not CGI-glossy, not AI-surreal.
- **Aspect ratio:** 3:2 landscape (horizontal). Keep the subject centred enough
  that a 16:9 crop still works.
- **Lighting:** soft, large diffused key light from the upper left with a subtle
  fill from the right. Gentle, controlled specular highlights running along the
  aluminium edges — aluminium should read as brushed satin metal, not chrome mirror.
  No harsh flash, no blown highlights, no heavy dramatic shadows.
- **Background:** clean, calm, warm off-white to very light cool grey seamless
  studio backdrop (around #FBFAF7 to #EFF3F8). Slight natural falloff and a soft
  contact shadow under the product. Absolutely no clutter.
- **Colour palette:** the natural silver of mill-finish and anodised aluminium,
  supported by the brand colours — deep navy (#14345C) and muted gold (#A67D26) —
  appearing only subtly, e.g. a dark navy surface the parts rest on, or one
  faintly gold-tinted anodised profile. Restrained and premium, never gaudy.
- **Composition:** a small, deliberate arrangement of real parts. Slight angle,
  around 30–40 degrees off axis, showing the cut ends of the profiles, because the
  cross-section is what a buyer recognises. Shallow-to-medium depth of field: the
  front of the subject is tack sharp, the far end falls off softly.
- **Mood:** industrial precision, export-grade quality, quiet confidence. Think a
  German engineering catalogue, not a stock-photo factory floor.
- **No text of any kind.** No labels, no logos, no brand names, no watermarks, no
  dimension callouts, no numbers printed on the parts. Website text will sit over
  and around these images, so the frame must stay clean.
- **No people, no hands, no faces.**

## THE FOUR IMAGES

### IMAGE 1 of 4 — ARCHITECTURAL PROFILES
*Category: curtain walls, windows, and façades.*

Extruded aluminium sections used in glazing and cladding systems: curtain-wall
mullions and transoms, window and door frame sections, sashes, pressure plates and
glazing beads. These are the profiles that hold glass in a building. Their
cross-sections are complex and hollow, with integral glazing pockets, gasket races
(small grooves that hold rubber seals), thermal-break channels, and screw ports
(small round bosses inside the profile that take self-tapping screws). Typical alloy
6063-T5, wall thickness 1.2–3.0 mm, mill finish or clear/coloured anodised.

**Shot:** a tight group of three to five architectural profile offcuts, roughly
200–300 mm long, standing and lying at varied angles with their intricate
cross-section ends facing the camera so the internal chambers, glazing pockets and
screw ports are clearly visible. Mix the finishes: one clear-anodised silver, one
matte black or bronze anodised, one mill finish. Optionally include a short length
of black EPDM gasket seated in one of the races, and a thin edge of glass sitting in
a glazing pocket, to make the purpose obvious. Every edge crisp and deburred.

### IMAGE 2 of 4 — INDUSTRIAL EXTRUSIONS
*Category: T-slot framing, heat sinks, enclosures.*

Functional extrusions for automation, tooling, and thermal management: modular
T-slot aluminium framing in 20, 30, 40 and 45 series with 6/8/10 mm slots, finned
extruded heat sinks for power electronics and LED drivers, enclosure and rack
framing with panel grooves, and machine-guard sections. Typical alloy 6061-T6 or
6063-T5, often supplied cut, drilled and tapped to drawing.

**Shot:** a compact arrangement combining the three signature products. A 40×40 mm
T-slot profile cut to roughly 250 mm standing with its four-slot cross-section
facing the camera, slots and centre bore clearly readable. Next to it, a finned
heat-sink extrusion — a solid base with a comb of tall parallel fins, clear
anodised — angled so the fin array catches the light and reads as a repeating
rhythm. Behind or beside them, a short piece of rack/enclosure profile with a panel
groove. Add one small technical cue: a couple of T-slot nuts and a hex-socket bolt
resting on the surface, and a drilled-and-tapped hole visible on one cut end. The
parts may rest on a dark navy matte surface for contrast.

### IMAGE 3 of 4 — CUSTOM DIE DEVELOPMENT
*Category: your drawing, extruded.*

This segment is about tooling, not a catalogue part. The customer sends a
cross-section drawing (DWG, DXF, STEP); we design the extrusion die, simulate the
flow, extrude a trial sample, correct the die, and then run production. The
recognisable objects are the **extrusion die itself** — a thick, heavy, round
hardened steel disc roughly 200–300 mm in diameter and 60–100 mm thick, dark
grey-black nitrided steel, with a precisely machined profile-shaped aperture cut
through the centre — and the **aluminium profile that came out of it**.

**Shot:** the round steel extrusion die lying flat or propped at a slight angle in
the foreground, its machined aperture facing the camera so the shaped opening is
the hero of the frame. Resting alongside it, a short offcut of the aluminium
profile whose cross-section exactly matches that aperture, so the viewer instantly
understands the relationship between the tool and the part. Underneath or partly
behind, a crisp white engineering drawing sheet showing a dimensioned technical
cross-section in fine navy line work — dimension lines, arrows, section hatching —
but **no legible text or numbers**, just believable drafting linework. The contrast
between dark industrial steel, bright aluminium, and the pale drawing is the point
of the image.

### IMAGE 4 of 4 — STANDARD SHAPES
*Category: angles, channels, tubes, flat bars.*

Stock catalogue cross-sections sold with no die cost and no tooling wait: equal and
unequal L-angles, C and U channels, round tubes, square and rectangular tubes, flat
bars, square bars, and solid rounds. Sizes such as 20×20 to 100×100 angle, Ø12 to
Ø100 round tube, 10×3 to 100×12 flat bar. Alloys 6061 / 6063 / 6082, usually mill
finish, lengths up to 6 m, sold by tonnage to fabricators and stockists.

**Shot:** a neat, deliberately ordered bundle of simple mill-finish aluminium stock
sections — an L-angle, a C-channel, a round tube, a square tube, a rectangular tube
and a flat bar — cut to roughly equal 250–300 mm lengths and stacked or fanned so
that all six cross-section ends face the camera in a clean row or cluster. The
appeal here is inventory and variety: simple geometry, repetition, consistent
natural satin aluminium, honest mill finish with its faint extrusion lines. Tidy and
warehouse-precise, like a stockist's sample set laid out for a buyer.
