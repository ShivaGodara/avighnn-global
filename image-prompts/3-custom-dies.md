## THE SUBJECT — CUSTOM DIE DEVELOPMENT

This card is about **tooling**, not a catalogue part. The customer sends a
cross-section drawing (DWG, DXF, STEP); we design the extrusion die, simulate the
metal flow, extrude a trial sample, correct the die, and then run production. The
message the picture must carry is: *your drawing becomes a tool, and the tool makes
your profile.*

**Important — this is NOT a window or curtain-wall image.** No glass, no gaskets, no
window frames. The hero object is a steel tool.

**Equally important — the aluminium profile in this image must NOT be a T-slot
framing extrusion.** No square profile with T-shaped slots on its faces, no centre
bore, no modular framing section of any kind. That shape belongs to a different
card in this series and must not be repeated here. The profile shown here has to
look **bespoke and one-off** — a section designed for a single customer, not
something you could order from a catalogue.

The two recognisable objects are:

- **The extrusion die** — a thick, heavy, round hardened steel disc roughly
  200–300 mm in diameter and 60–100 mm thick, dark grey-black nitrided steel with a
  faint machining sheen, with a precisely machined profile-shaped aperture cut
  through the centre.
- **The aluminium profile that came out of it** — a short bright extruded offcut
  whose cross-section exactly matches that aperture, opening for opening.

**The custom profile's cross-section:** an asymmetric, irregular shape that reads
as purpose-built. Roughly rectangular overall but deliberately unbalanced — two or
three hollow chambers of clearly *different* sizes, one heavy thick-walled lobe on
one side, a thin flange or fin cantilevered off the other, a small curved or
radiused feature somewhere, and one or two screw ports. Nothing about it should be
symmetrical or repeating. A viewer should look at it and think "someone drew that
for one specific job", not "that's a standard section".

**The shot:** the round steel die lying flat or propped at a slight angle in the
foreground, its machined aperture facing the camera so the shaped opening is the
hero of the frame. Resting alongside it, the matching aluminium profile offcut,
positioned so a viewer instantly reads the relationship between tool and part —
**the aperture in the die and the end of the profile must be the same shape**, so
the pairing is obvious at a glance. Underneath or partly behind, a crisp white
engineering drawing sheet showing a dimensioned technical cross-section in fine navy
line work — dimension lines, arrows, leader lines and section hatching — and **the
drawn cross-section must be that same bespoke shape again**, so drawing, tool and
part are visibly one design in three forms. The drawing carries **no legible text
and no numbers**, just believable drafting linework. The contrast between dark
industrial steel, bright aluminium, and the pale drawing sheet is the point of the
image.

## STYLE

- **Medium:** photorealistic commercial product photography. Sharp, clean, high
  detail. Not illustration, not a 3D render look, not CGI-glossy, not AI-surreal.
- **Aspect ratio:** 3:2 landscape, 1536 × 1024.
- **Lighting:** soft, large diffused key light from the upper left with a subtle
  fill from the right. Gentle, controlled specular highlights — the aluminium reads
  as brushed satin metal, not chrome mirror, and the steel die reads as matte dark
  tool steel. No harsh flash, no blown highlights, no heavy dramatic shadows.
- **Background:** seamless, very light cool neutral grey studio backdrop (around
  #F2F2F2 to #F7F7F7), gentle natural falloff, soft contact shadow under the parts.
  No clutter of any kind.
- **Composition:** the die angled so its aperture is legible rather than edge-on.
  Medium depth of field: the front of the subject is tack sharp, the far end falls
  off softly. **Keep the entire arrangement inside the frame with clear margin on
  all four sides — nothing may run off or bleed past an edge.**
- **Mood:** industrial precision, export-grade quality, quiet confidence. A German
  engineering catalogue, not a stock-photo factory floor.
- **No text of any kind** — no labels, logos, brand names, watermarks, dimension
  figures, or part numbers stamped on the die. Website text will sit over this
  image.
- **No people, no hands, no faces.**
